# Literature review and research-gap audit

**Corpus cut-off:** 1 November 2026. **Status:** close reading of the supplied workbook/PDF corpus plus targeted primary-source refresh for recent explanation-robustness and process-triage work. This is a working review; one close SSRN preprint is available only at abstract level, and novelty is not certified.

## Corpus audit and evidence limits

The workbook contains 30 main entries and 3 extras. The inventory contains 28 supplied PDFs mapped to 28 entries; five records have no local PDF (IDs 24, 28, 29, E2, E3). The complete workbook-derived bibliography, abstracts, setting labels, code links, PDF mapping, and full-text status are in [`literature_matrix.csv`](literature_matrix.csv). Its abstracts and venue/citation notes are discovery aids rather than authoritative bibliographic evidence. Citation counts in the workbook are a dated snapshot and are not used as evidence of quality. Several entries are adjacent IIoT/network IDS rather than CPS process-level IDS; these should not be pooled into a single performance comparison.

The 19 directly tagged ICS/CPS main entries cover graph/recurrent process anomaly detection; federated, privacy-preserving, microgrid, and IIoT detectors; residual and process-aware explanations; XAI robustness; temporal Shapley; and adaptive mitigation. Eight additional main entries cover adjacent transport/IIoT/energy settings, and three are method-only network IDS. Nine main entries were moved from the workbook's extras list; venue strength is not a substitute for methodological quality, and those entries should be screened using the same evidence standards.

## Synthesis by method family

### 1. Detector-integrated structure and residual explanations

GDN learns multivariate relationships for forecasting and uses graph attention/residual signals; GRN adds recurrent temporal modeling. Other supplied work uses correlation structures, logical representations, autoencoders, or process-aware models. These papers establish that learned dependencies and residual channels can support CPS anomaly detection and localization. They also make “use a graph/recurrent detector and show its attention” an occupied contribution. Attention or residual magnitude is not automatically a faithful explanation: prediction error identifies model surprise, not necessarily the adversary's manipulated component. Results from SWaT/WADI-style benchmarks also depend strongly on thresholding and attack annotation.

### 2. Post-hoc feature attribution and explanation comparisons

The corpus includes SHAP, Kernel/Gradient SHAP, LIME, saliency, integrated-gradient-like methods, MMD-Critic, and multiple-explainer comparisons. WaXAI and the smart-substation study measure explanation properties including faithfulness/stability/runtime; FedeX studies edge resource costs; microgrid papers examine different explanatory summaries. This means a paper that merely compares several explainers on one ICS dataset, reports a few example plots, or adds an edge-runtime table is unlikely to be novel.

The closest methodological overlap is [Fung et al. (NDSS 2024)](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/attributions-for-ml-based-ics-anomaly-detection-from-theory-to-practice/). Its full paper was rechecked against the official text on 1 November 2026. It explicitly evaluates whether attribution rankings identify the ground-truth manipulated sensors and actuators in SWaT and WADI attacks, plus physically realizable TEP simulator manipulations. For each detected attack it uses the first practical detection point and the preceding 50-step input window, ranks the manipulated feature(s), and reports AvgRank; it also compares a best-guess timing and an attribution ensemble. It analyzes 47 real attacks comprising 67 feature manipulations and reports timing, attack-property, and categorical-actuator effects. Therefore, evaluation at the first alarm against true manipulated features is directly occupied. New datasets, a coarser target taxonomy, or an evidence-provenance table alone do not establish a high-impact novelty claim. Any evaluation-only contribution must show a materially different question and utility (for example, calibrated abstention under genuinely ambiguous target evidence) and should be treated as lower priority unless it yields a result unavailable from this benchmark. A method contribution needs to distinguish itself from detector-score faithfulness, process-aware LRP, JoCE, and AR-Pro, and must be tested with a version-matched executable process model rather than a statistical normal-data proxy.

### 3. Temporal explanations and counterfactuals

[ShaTS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.future.2025.108178) groups time-dependent Shapley contributions on time series and evaluates sensor/process localization and resource cost on SWaT. Generic temporal Shapley or process grouping is therefore not new. [JoCE](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2026.113324) formulates model-agnostic time-series anomaly counterfactuals as joint time-feature edits with temporal consistency and evaluates on four benchmarks, including SWaT. Generic sparse, temporally coherent counterfactuals for TSAD are occupied territory. A CPS counterfactual paper must add a demonstrable security-specific property such as explicit attacker reachability, sensor-versus-actuator actionability, plant transition constraints, and independently annotated target validation.

**Supplementary direct prior work: AR-Pro.** Ji et al.'s [NeurIPS 2024 AR-Pro paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1d3591b6746204b332acb464b775d38d-Abstract-Conference.html) develops diffusion-based anomaly repairs and detector-centric criteria for overall anomaly-score improvement, similarity, localized improvement, and non-degradation. It evaluates HAI, SWaT, and WADI with 100-step sliding windows and GPT-2/Llama detectors, and the authors publish [the implementation](https://github.com/xjiae/arpro). The article explicitly says its iterative guidance tends toward the properties but does not guarantee their satisfaction. The paper reports 86 HAI features, while this project uses 79 numeric process tags from HAI 21.03; we have not verified that AR-Pro's processed HAI release matches HAI 21.03. The criteria are about the fitted detector's score and attribution mask, not a proof of plant feasibility or attacker capability; its reported evaluation also does not provide exact attack-target hit-rate metrics. AR-Pro means a new manuscript cannot claim that repair-style counterfactuals, formal detector-side repair criteria, or HAI counterfactual evaluation are themselves novel. A possible gap is the additional modeling and validation of scenario-specific attacker actions and documented target components, but this remains a candidate contribution pending a fair temporal-window comparison.

The official implementation is not directly runnable in this workspace as checked: its time-series evaluation loads model checkpoints from developer-specific absolute paths and invokes CUDA directly; this Python environment has no PyTorch installed. It expects preprocessed HAI data and trains a separate GPT-2/Llama detector and Diffusion-TS repair model. We therefore record AR-Pro as a critical un-reproduced baseline, not as a result of our experiments. Adapting its metrics to the current one-step PCA/RobustZ models would not reproduce its temporal-window diffusion method and must be labeled a metric-level comparison only.

JoCE's use of the phrase “causally relevant” does not itself demonstrate causal identification. A new paper should reserve “causal” for an identified structural causal model and validated interventions; otherwise use “counterfactual” or “process-constrained recourse.”

### 4. Robustness, privacy, federation, and response

The corpus includes federated explainability for ICS, privacy-preserving CPS threat detection, smart-grid false-data injection robustness, and adaptive mitigation. A general claim that XAI improves model robustness, privacy, or response is already broad and unsupported without an explicit attacker and measurable mechanism. These themes are better treated as boundary conditions or future work unless one is central to the method and validated end-to-end.

### 5. Newest broad frameworks and comparison studies

[XAI-CPS (Computer Networks, 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comnet.2026.112320) combines a hybrid spatiotemporal detector, physics-informed LRP, expert/response components, and multiple CPS data sources. The publisher abstract reports mean F1 92.9%; the supplied PDF conclusion reports 94.7%. This unresolved version/internal discrepancy means neither number should be quoted comparatively until the version and calculation are reconciled. The broad phrase “physics-aware XAI for CPS” substantially overlaps this work.

[Oinonen and Morsi (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsr.2026.113087) is labeled “Review” by the publisher but reports an experimental comparison of seven explainers in a smart-substation IEC 61850 testbed, including faithfulness, stability, runtime, and parameter sensitivity. It occupies the broad explainer-comparison space despite its document-type label. Treat it by its reported methods and evidence, not only its database category.

**Direct HAI overlap: RAID (Moccardi et al., *Sensors*, 2026).** The [RAID article](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26216727) uses HAI 21.03 and combines regime-aware Isolation Forest detection, physics-informed feature engineering, structured rule explanations, LLM-generated reports, and RAG over prior incidents. Its explanation audit covers 26 anomalous samples (12 within attacks and 14 false alarms); 1,040 generated propositions are compared with the Isolation Forest's mathematical decision paths, with 87% judged to match exactly by a Judge Agent. This is direct overlap in dataset, operational motivation, and explanation evaluation. Its reference is the detector's own rule path, however, and the reported endpoint is rule-to-text fidelity rather than independently documented attacked-tag localization at first alarm. A five-seed, normal-only Isolation Forest reference has now been added on the same 79 HAI process tags and exact-target intervals: its Hit@5 was 0.297 versus 0.083 under an event-matched random-rank null, but it did not outperform PCA-TEMPORAL grouped ablation (paired difference -0.014, 95% event-bootstrap CI [-0.214, 0.186], Holm-adjusted $p=0.945$). This is only a same-dataset detector-family comparator; it does not reproduce RAID's regime discovery or 330-feature physics pipeline. The manuscript must not imply that explanation auditing on HAI, rule fidelity, or physics-informed feature engineering is new, and any distinction from RAID remains provisional until target labels and detector-rule fidelity are compared directly.

The adjacent network-IDS literature has also moved beyond visual inspection. Maseno, Sun, and Wang's [2026 reliability audit](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11416-026-00664-7) evaluates SHAP-permutation agreement, sufficiency/comprehensiveness, retraining stability, and spurious-feature injection on UNSW-NB15 and IoT-ToN. A 2026 IEEE comparison [tests SHAP and LIME robustness under adversarial perturbations on CIC-IDS2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/AEECT69724.2026.11657835). These studies make a general “reliable XAI audit” or adversarial stability metric an insufficient novelty claim. Their inputs and labels are network traffic; they do not establish event-time target validity for process telemetry. The remaining CPS-specific opportunity must be validated against documented physical-component targets at alert time, while clearly separating target agreement from physical feasibility.

**Additional 2026 overlap found in the conference refresh (1 November 2026).** The official IEEE CSR 2026 accepted-paper list includes Nugraha and Bauschert's *DARE: A Framework for Drift-Aware Robust Explanations in Unsupervised Intrusion Detection* ([official program](https://www.ieee-csr.org/2026-conference-program/)). The program establishes its title and authors, but a DOI, abstract, and full paper were not located in this audit; no method or result is inferred from the title. Separately, TU Chemnitz's institutional bibliography identifies the peer-reviewed IFIP Networking 2026 paper *E2D2: A Modular Framework for Explanation-Based Drift Detection in Unsupervised Network Intrusion Detection*, DOI [10.23919/ifipnetworking70592.2026.11578962](https://doi.org/10.23919/ifipnetworking70592.2026.11578962). Its [author-maintained public implementation](https://github.com/DLTeamTUC/E2D2) describes explanation fingerprints, an Explanation Drift Intensity signal, and a 48-configuration study across five network-traffic benchmarks and four drift scenarios. The implementation documents CIC-DDoS2019 subsets, CICIoT2023, and 5GNIDD; this is adjacent network IDS work, not evidence of CPS process-tag target localization. The paper PDF itself was not available in this audit, so claims about its results are limited to the authors' public repository documentation. These works rule out drift-aware explanations, explanation-based drift detection, and generic robustness of unsupervised IDS explanations as new directions. The current candidate is narrower: independently sourced CPS attack-target agreement at the first alarm, with explicit evidence provenance/resolution and abstention. Whether E2D2 or DARE evaluates that target-validity endpoint cannot be established without the full texts; obtain and compare those papers before making any novelty claim. Stability/robustness should remain a secondary measured property and not be presented as the main novel contribution.

**Implementation-level E2D2 comparison.** The pinned notebook blob was inspected, including source code and saved cell outputs. The alignment endpoint compares top-ranked alarm-window features with synthetic `drift_features_names`/`shift_features_names`; it does not score independently sourced CPS attack-target tags. Its feature selector also uses attack labels from the full table before the chronological benign-reference/stream split (ANOVA on all rows and labels; SHAP values evaluated on all rows). The subsequent scaler is reference-only, but that does not remove the earlier label-informed selection. Under strict online/unsupervised evaluation, this is a material leakage concern; an intended offline label-assisted feature-selection regime would need separate disclosure. The notebook's embedded output reports mean Jaccard 0.193 and mean GT-feature recall 0.200 for 15 non-empty dataset/scenario alignment results; these are source-reported outputs and were not reproduced here. See [`E2D2_IMPLEMENTATION_AUDIT.md`](E2D2_IMPLEMENTATION_AUDIT.md) for code-path evidence and implications. This narrows the comparison, but does not certify novelty for CPS target agreement.

**Direct comparison with GRIDGUARD-AI (primary article and supplement inspected 1 November 2026).** Kamran and Saman's 2026 Research Square paper ([full text](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-11447297/v1.html), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11447297/v1)) is a close benchmark and framing overlap: it uses Data4Cyber, eight scenario-held-out folds, 20-step/141-variable windows, RF/TCN/AE detection, deterministic SAFE/AUTO ALERT/HUMAN REVIEW escalation, and bounded evidence-retrieval agents. The [official Zenodo supplement](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23512250) is authored by the same researchers and includes an executed Kaggle notebook, supplement S1, and summary CSV. It reports 14,202 overlapping windows; 84.52% are sent to simulated human review. The paper and artifact characterize the 98.37% attack escalation as routing rather than detector recall, disclose test-informed revision of the final routing policy, and state there was no independent end-to-end replay, live operator trial, or grid actuation. Notebook cell 83 reconstructs one held-out RF case and provides its top-10 TreeSHAP ranking using anonymous feature indices; neither that cell nor the audited supplement evaluates event-level explanation agreement with independently documented register/tag targets. The notebook is not a clean independent rerun. It cites Data4Cyber v2 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20004612), while our schema audit uses the corrected v3 archive. This narrows a defensible distinction to independently audited, first-alarm target agreement with explicit evidence tiers, not scenario-held-out explainable detection, a three-way assurance gate, or agentic investigation. The distinction remains a candidate gap, not certified novelty, and still needs matched baseline comparison and independent replication. The artifact provenance and scope audit is documented in `GRIDGUARD_AI_SUPPLEMENT_AUDIT.md`.

The supplied [XAI-CPS repository](https://github.com/mazharulmd/XAI-CPS) itself is the source corpus, not the work being proposed; the local spreadsheet's recommendation is not an independent novelty check.

### 7. Explanation-integrity attacks and process-system security (1 November 2026)

The direction “measure explanation robustness against adversarial inputs” is also occupied in adjacent security literature. Mia and Pritom's [2025 arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03623) tests six explanation-manipulation attacks on SHAP/LIME/Integrated Gradients in four tabular security settings, including IDS, and includes prediction-preserving explanation steering. Maseno, Sun, and Wang's peer-reviewed [2026 *International Journal of Information Security* article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10207-026-01299-x) evaluates SHAP drift and top-k overlap as adversarial-evasion signals for recurrent IDS on ToN-IoT, with a learned secondary detector. Rajhans and Khawarey's [February](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06395) and [July 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01679) preprints jointly measure detector robustness and attribution drift on network/tabular security data, including non-differentiable tree models. Therefore, generic explanation drift, top-k overlap, and explanation-based attack detection are not new contributions.

The process-security overlap is separate but substantial. Pozdnyakov et al.'s [2024 TEP attack/defense benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13502) studies six adversarial attacks and five defenses against MLP/GRU/TCN fault-diagnosis models; Shaik and Das's peer-reviewed [2026 TEP anomaly-detector study](https://doi.org/10.1002/cjce.70202) evaluates adversarial attacks against process-system detectors. Ma et al.'s [September 2026 *Sensors* article](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26175585) evaluates alarm-gated, physical-consistency triage between process faults and measurement-integrity anomalies on TEP and a wind-farm simulator; it includes matched sensor-fault/attack controls and abstention. Skrodelis and Romanovs' peer-reviewed [2026 ICS audit](https://doi.org/10.37394/23203.2026.21.5) evaluates operational sensitivity to window stride, thresholding, SHAP behavior, drift, and latency on CIC Modbus, TU Wien, and IEC-104 data. These rule out novelty claims for generic process adversarial robustness, physical-versus-measurement triage/abstention, and operational sensitivity audits.

A close unresolved overlap is Cullen and Carrington's [June 2026 SSRN preprint](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6979658). Its public abstract names SWaT, WADI, and CIC-IDS; post-hoc explanations including SHAP, Integrated Gradients, and counterfactuals; an explanation-aware adversarial attack framework; and a proposed sanitization/differential-privacy defense. SSRN returned 403 when the full text was requested, so attack objectives, evaluation protocol, code, and exact relation to the CPS process-target question remain unchecked. This should be treated as a high-risk prior-art lead, not as verified evidence of the paper's detailed results. The candidate alarm-preserving explanation-displacement question cannot be selected until that full text and its citation chain are reviewed.

The potentially distinct intersection remains a hypothesis: explanation-space manipulation under an explicitly declared CPS measurement-layer capability model, with the plant's latent state separated from values delivered to the IDS, and with a process-consistency signal used to flag compromised explanations. Existing HAI/BATADAL/Data4Cyber telemetry can support retrospective tests but cannot alone prove attacker reachability or physical feasibility. No “first” claim is warranted. A simulator such as TEP may support controlled generation, but the simulator version, API, scenario semantics, and independent validation need to be established before designing a confirmatory experiment.

### 6. Literature and dataset refresh: newly available 2026 evidence

The supplied workbook/PDF snapshot does not include several relevant 2026 releases found in a fresh source search. **Data4Cyber** is a peer-reviewed IEEE CSR 2026 dataset paper and a public CC BY 4.0 v3 archive ([conference program](https://www.ieee-csr.org/2026-conference-program/), [Zenodo v3](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22953576)). The archive README describes eight scenario folders (S0--S6 plus an alternate PV run), 14,354 one-second process rows, six primary attack families, process activity/phase labels, attack-event logs, packet labels and PCAPs, and register/endpoint-to-process-variable mappings. Its phase labels and mappings create a promising basis for testing whether an alarm explanation recovers documented attack stage and targeted cyber/physical variables. The six families are not six independent plants or a large scenario sample; the Zenodo record states that a companion publication reports results across twelve IPAL detectors. The exact paper protocol and its relationship to the corrected v3 labels have not yet been verified from the companion paper's full text, so those published numbers are not treated as matched baselines. The upstream IPAL framework supports both message and process-state detectors, but supervised methods would change the current normal-only estimand. Our constrained GeCo-AR1 adapter explicitly limits search to a one-step self-lag model, imputes from training-normal data, and calibrates on normal-only data; it is not full GeCo. See the dedicated compatibility audit. A new study must group the S1-alt repeat with S1, use family-disjoint evaluation, report small-sample limits, and contribute an explanation-specific validity question rather than claim a new dataset, generic multimodal detector, or extra baseline sweep. A checksum-verified audit of 107 selected metadata and telemetry files reproduces all 14,354 rows: 7,586 active and 6,768 benign. The Zenodo v3 landing page still lists the pre-correction 5,880/8,474 split; the archive `CHANGES.md` documents S3 label correction, and the verified v3 README/CSV labels are used here. Ground-truth granularity varies: S1/S1-alt and S2 name asset-level targets, S3 identifies a meter IP/unit/register path joinable to telemetry, S4/S5 use generic `mitm` phase targets with device groups in the scenario READMEs, and S6 logs an MQTT topic but its packet capture contains no MQTT messages.

The process-only family-held-out baselines give equal-weight mean average precision 0.728 for PCA residual, 0.681 for RobustZ, and 0.663 for a normal-only 500-tree Isolation Forest. The PCA-minus-RobustZ AP difference has raw exact sign-flip $p=0.03125$ and adjusted $p=0.5625$ under the expanded 18-contrast Holm family; nominal normal FPR thresholds also transfer poorly to some held-out scenario files, reaching 32.3% test FPR at a 5% nominal setting. A constrained GeCo-AR1 reference (self-lag linear subset, training-normal imputation, normal-only threshold calibration) gives mean AP 0.620; its paired comparisons against PCA and RobustZ do not establish superiority after correction. This is an adapted subset, not a reproduction of full GeCo or the twelve IPAL results mentioned by the Data4Cyber release. Across 18 paired detector/metric/threshold contrasts, no Forest-versus-PCA/RobustZ difference survives Holm correction; the Forest does not improve AP. Its explanation is grouped black-box score ablation, not native attribution. These exploratory results show that the candidate paper must treat detector calibration and regime transfer as part of explanation evaluation. First-alarm target counts remain very small; see `docs/research/DATA4CYBER_PRELIMINARY_RESULTS.md` and `docs/research/DATA4CYBER_IPAL_BASELINE_COMPATIBILITY.md`.

**ProvICS** adds an even richer, but much heavier and less mature, option: its [2026 arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05989) and [Hugging Face dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/trucyberlab/multimodal-ICS-provenance) describe synchronized host provenance, PLC-edge provenance, Modbus semantics, and physical telemetry over 32 labeled events and 20 ATT&CK ICS techniques. The card reports 30.2 GB and a CC BY-NC 4.0 restriction. It is a preprint/dataset release, not a peer-reviewed XAI benchmark, and the graph scale makes it inappropriate as an immediate default experiment. It is a conditional external replication candidate if selected streams can be handled reproducibly and the noncommercial terms fit the intended publication and code release.

This refresh changes the feasible research plan: retain the completed HAI/BATADAL audit as separate pilot evidence and continue the evidence-granularity-aware Data4Cyber evaluation. A plausible specific gap is **first-alarm explanation validity against independent attack evidence**, distinct from matching generated prose to a detector's own rules: separately test whether a frozen detector's explanation identifies the documented attack phase, mapped process variable/register, or device/asset group at the strongest evidence level actually available, while preventing phase labels and target mappings from entering fitting or explanation generation. This is not a novelty claim. RAID directly overlaps HAI 21.03, regime-aware Isolation Forest and rule-grounded explanation evaluation; GRIDGUARD-AI overlaps Data4Cyber and scenario-held-out assurance, but its inspected supplement contains only one anonymous-feature RF SHAP case and no event-target localization. A standard Isolation Forest family comparator now exists on HAI with normal-only training, first-alarm target auditing and the same exact-tag event denominator, but it does not reproduce RAID's process feature pipeline or rule-to-text endpoint. The candidate distinction remains event-time agreement with separately sourced target evidence and explicit evidence-resolution/coverage reporting. The full row schema and labels are audited, but exact target granularity remains uneven. Next compare external target agreement with detector-rule fidelity directly; reproduce a meaningful subset of the released IPAL baselines at matched alert operating points; resolve S4/S5 endpoint ambiguity; and assess whether this distinction adds value beyond NDSS attribution evaluation and RAID.

## Cross-cutting unresolved challenges

1. **Alarm-time mismatch:** many evaluations explain an attack onset or chosen peak rather than the actual alarm point; delayed detection can change which features appear important.
2. **Root-cause ground truth:** overall attack labels and process-stage labels do not identify the manipulated sensor, actuator, or causal chain. Stage localization must not be called root-cause attribution.
3. **Counterfactual actionability:** an input edit that changes a score can be outside attacker capability, physically impossible, or unsafe. A nearest-normal distance is only a statistical proxy and does not certify process feasibility.
4. **False-alarm burden under shift:** a nominal validation FPR does not determine test alarm-episode rate, operator burden, or cross-period calibration. Report per-file and event-level false alarms, not only pooled point F1.
5. **Explanation evaluation validity:** feature deletion can create off-manifold samples; rank stability under small random noise is narrow; fidelity, localization, plausibility, and operator usefulness are distinct constructs.
6. **Temporal and categorical channels:** time, actuator discreteness, control loops, process phases, and inter-tag dependencies are often incompletely represented in generic tabular explainers.
7. **Operational evidence:** inference-only latency is not end-to-end alert handling; explanation queues, concurrent alarms, memory, edge hardware, and deadlines are rarely established together.
8. **Reproducibility and benchmark leakage:** papers vary in attack-window scoring, threshold selection, point adjustment, random-window splits, normalization, and dataset versions. These choices can dominate results.
9. **Generalization and uncertainty:** few studies establish explanation performance across plants, attack families, operating regimes, and multiple independent scenarios with uncertainty intervals.

## Novelty boundary and source verification

Current verified boundary: temporal attribution, temporal counterfactual generation, physics-aware attribution, explanation stability/faithfulness/runtime comparisons, attack-grounded attribution benchmarking, adversarial explanation manipulation/detection in generic IDS, and adversarial TEP fault diagnosis have all been published in closely related forms. A possible contribution lies only in a more specific joint endpoint: alarm-preserving explanation displacement under a disclosed CPS measurement-layer capability model, evaluated with latent process state and received telemetry separated. The SSRN preprint may already cover much of this question and has not been inspected in full. Do not write a novelty claim until that source, its references, and a fresh scholarly-index/citation-chain search are reviewed, and a direct implementation-level comparison is designed.

The closest recent claims were checked against publisher/venue pages and the NDSS paper page. For benchmark provenance, use dataset maintainers rather than secondary summaries: [HAI repository](https://github.com/icsdataset/hai), [BATADAL data page](https://www.batadal.net/data.html), and [iTrust SWaT page](https://www.sutd.edu.sg/itrust/itrust-labs/datasets/dataset-characteristics/swat/). Dataset release, terms, and labels must be rechecked before manuscript submission.

## Dataset evidence and annotation limits

HAI 21.03 is collected from a hardware-in-the-loop testbed with boiler, turbine, water-treatment, and simulator processes. Its dataset paper describes setpoint, process-variable, and control-variable measurements and states attack-target/time metadata were used to produce labels; the maintainers' technical manual lists attack IDs, times, controllers, and target points. We have now aligned all 50 manual entries to unique CSV intervals by their minute-resolution timestamps and verified that their documented controller stages match the P1–P3 CSV labels. However, 21 entries contain at least one target-point name absent from the CSV schema, with plausible but unverified aliases; one manual duration differs from the CSV label span by 40 seconds. Exact tag-localization evaluation is therefore restricted to the 29 intervals whose complete target list matches exact CSV column names. This is scenario-target agreement, not causal root-cause validation. See [Shin et al. (CSET 2021)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3474718.3474719), the [maintainer repository](https://github.com/icsdataset/hai), and the local [mapping audit](HAI_ATTACK_TARGET_MAPPING.md).

BATADAL's official page states Dataset03 is normal-only, Dataset04 is only partially/approximately labeled, and the official competition test file has no labels. Our downloaded Dataset04 specifically contains 219 positive `ATT_FLAG=1` rows and 3,958 unknown `-999` rows, with no confirmed negatives; it can support approximate-positive recall and delay but not precision, F1, or held-out false-positive estimates. The official [BATADAL FAQ](https://www.batadal.net/faq.html) supplies day-level pump-component targets for these five positive intervals, so we now evaluate post-hoc component localization. It does not give exact manipulated channels; only five intervals are eligible, and their labels remain approximate. The FAQ also states the published EPANET demand patterns differ from those used to generate BATADAL and the generating demands are unavailable, so the model file cannot validate exact telemetry transitions.
