# Initial Research-Gap Assessment and Direction Ranking

**Project:** Explainable AI for Intrusion Detection in Cyber-Physical Systems
**Assessment date:** 31 October 2026
**Status:** Updated assessment of the supplied corpus plus a 31 October 2026 source refresh. A first exploratory Data4Cyber detector/explanation audit has now run; this is still not a novelty certification or a final benchmark.

## Executive assessment

The topic is active and already crowded. The defensible opportunity is not another high-accuracy classifier with SHAP/LIME, nor a generic comparison of explanation methods. The repository already contains work on graph and recurrent anomaly detectors, temporal Shapley explanations, attribution evaluation on real ICS attacks, edge runtime, adversarial robustness, and physics-aware explanations. Recent literature adds counterfactual time-series explanations and broad XAI comparisons for smart substations. Any manuscript that claims those ideas are individually new would face immediate reviewer objections.

**Strongest currently executable candidate:** evaluate **evidence-granularity-aware explanation-to-target validity at the first alarm** on Data4Cyber v3, separating exact register-to-tag evidence, documented device-group evidence, and coarse asset/phase evidence. The primary GRIDGUARD-AI manuscript has now been inspected: it shares Data4Cyber, scenario-held-out evaluation, and explainable assurance, but its reported explanation evidence is one anonymous-feature TreeSHAP case rather than event-level register/tag target localization. The distinction must therefore be narrowly tested as first-alarm explanation-to-target agreement with audited evidence tiers, not general explainability, scenario-held-out detection, or assurance routing; code/supplement comparison remains pending. The candidate asks whether detector explanations recover the strongest available independent target and phase evidence at first alarm, and how this changes across detector and feature-group choices. It does not claim modality fusion as novel. The full 164,148,991-byte archive passed its MD5 check; all 195 manifest-listed files were verified, and the split CSVs reproduce 14,354 rows and shows uneven ground truth: S3 has the clearest register-to-process-variable path; S1/S1-alt and S2 identify coarse assets and control registers without direct telemetry-tag mappings; S4/S5 give generic `mitm` phase targets with device groups described in scenario READMEs; S6 has an MQTT topic and process context but no MQTT packets in the released capture. The first family-held-out run of RobustZ/PCA shows threshold-transfer errors, and its small target-event counts are descriptive only. Six attack families from one testbed limit generalization; S1-alt remains grouped with S1. This candidate is a bounded evaluation contribution, not a novelty certification, causal claim, or validated process-model result.

The candidate is distinct in evaluation target from (i) attribution maps that rank influential inputs, (ii) ShaTS temporal Shapley grouping, (iii) JoCE time-feature counterfactuals, (iv) AR-Pro detector-centric anomaly repairs, and (v) physics-aware LRP. Multimodal IDS and XAI are already published, so modality fusion alone is not novel. GRIDGUARD-AI covers Data4Cyber scenario-held-out detection and explainable risk escalation; our narrower proposed question is first-alarm explanation agreement with separately audited cyber/physical target evidence at the strongest available granularity. The full text is now verified; verify its code and supplement before asserting a distinct contribution. The provisional contribution is a **leakage-free, first-alarm evaluation that reports explanation validity separately by independent evidence granularity**, with family-grouped scenario splits and explicit abstention for unresolved mappings. It must establish added value beyond NDSS attribution evaluation, GRIDGUARD-AI, and the released Data4Cyber detector baselines. This is a testable candidate, not a novelty claim.

### Evidence-based scope update (31 October 2026)

Maseno et al.'s August 2026 IDS reliability audit already combines attribution agreement, sufficiency/comprehensiveness, retraining stability, and spurious-feature tests on network-traffic datasets. A 2026 IEEE study also compares SHAP/LIME robustness under adversarial perturbations on CIC-IDS2017. A generic reliability audit or adversarial stability metric is therefore not a sufficient novelty claim. Neither study establishes event-time CPS target validity on process telemetry.

The original process-counterfactual direction remains unsupported by HAI/BATADAL numeric dynamics: HAI 21.03 has no validated numeric transition model; the later HAI 23.05 graph is topology-only and cross-release unmapped; and BATADAL's official FAQ says published demand patterns differ from the telemetry-generating demands. A fresh search identified Data4Cyber v3, which appears to improve target and cross-modal annotation availability but does not, from its README, establish executable process transition constraints. HAI's 29 exact-tag targets, the five approximate BATADAL pump-component targets, and Data4Cyber's attack-phase/register mappings now support a stronger candidate than the original audit-only direction, subject to direct schema validation. The existing HAI/BATADAL work remains pilot evidence, not a result for this new candidate.

The attacker-capability/plant-constrained counterfactual method remains the highest-impact *conditional* direction, but it is not executable on the present evidence. It should become the primary method paper only after obtaining a version-matched, independently specified process model and resolving matched AR-Pro/JoCE comparisons. The current pilot has not validated that method.

## What the supplied corpus covers

The workbook has one sheet (`Papers`) with 30 main studies and 3 extras. It classifies 19 main studies as directly ICS/CPS, 8 as adjacent IIoT/transport/energy, and 3 as method-only network IDS. Nine main studies were moved up from the extra list and the workbook itself labels their venues as comparatively weaker. The repository has 28 PDFs; five workbook records have no matching PDF among the supplied files. Citation counts are a dated snapshot and are not evidence of novelty or study quality.

The directly relevant methods include graph/recurrent anomaly detectors (GDN; GRN), autoencoders, attention-based IDS, federated/SHAP systems, interpretable rule learning, and explanation families including SHAP, LIME, saliency, integrated gradients, RISE, MMD-Critic, and physics-aware LRP. The most important prior work for scoping is:

- **Attributions for ML-based ICS Anomaly Detection (NDSS 2024):** evaluates multiple attribution methods against real manipulated features across detectors and datasets. It finds explanations can be poor at the alarm point, depend on attack timing and feature type, and struggle on categorical actuators; an ensemble helps. This rules out a novelty claim based merely on evaluating explanations against attack labels.
- **ShaTS (journal issue 2026; DOI registered 2025):** time-aware Shapley feature grouping on SWaT, with sensor/component localization and resource comparison. A generic temporal attribution proposal is not new.
- **Oinonen and Morsi (2026):** the publisher categorizes the paper as a *Review*, although it reports new comparisons of seven XAI methods on a smart-substation IDS/testbed. It measures faithfulness, stability, and runtime and notes generalizability/runtime as concerns. A multi-explainer comparison in a single ICS application is already occupied territory.
- **XAI-CPS (Computer Networks, 2026):** combines a spatiotemporal detector, physics-informed LRP, expert assessment, and response selection on several CPS datasets. A broad “physics-aware XAI-CPS framework” would overlap substantially; its reported metrics must be independently examined before making comparative claims.
- **JoCE (Pattern Recognition, September 2026):** model-agnostic counterfactual explanations for time-series anomaly detection, with temporal consistency and sparse joint time-feature masks, evaluated on four datasets including SWaT. Counterfactual explanations for time series, even with SWaT, are not new by themselves.
- **AR-Pro (NeurIPS 2024):** diffusion-based counterfactual repairs with detector-level properties for overall score improvement, similarity, localized improvement, and non-degradation. It evaluates time series on SWaT, WADI, and HAI and has a [public author repository](https://github.com/xjiae/arpro). The paper describes a 100-step window and 86 HAI features; the present HAI 21.03 adapter uses 79 numeric process tags, so the exact release/schema is not shown to match. AR-Pro guides repairs toward detector-centric properties; the paper says the iterative guidance does not guarantee their satisfaction. It does not validate attacker reachability, plant safety, or exact attack-target localization. Thus generic counterfactual anomaly repair and HAI-based evaluation are already occupied.
- **Adversarial robustness for XAI-based federated smart-grid FDI (2025):** already investigates evasion and gradient-inversion robustness. A generic claim that “XAI improves IDS robustness” would overlap.
- **WaXAI (CPSS 2024):** compares explanation approaches for SWaT detectors and discusses missed attacks, thresholds, and the delay/quality trade-off. Runtime and false-negative analysis are not new in isolation.

The workbook’s own novelty note says no inspected work combined several explanation methods, several deep detectors, several plant datasets, and edge testing against plant-damage deadlines. That is useful as a gap hypothesis, but the 2026 Oinonen paper, XAI-CPS, ShaTS, JoCE, and other recent publications make that exact formulation too weak. “More models × more explainers × more datasets” is not the recommended contribution.

### Why the current-workspace recommendation leads

The current results favor a **two-dataset evaluation design**, not a Data4Cyber-only target-validity paper. HAI 21.03 has 29 attack intervals with complete exact-tag target lists after strict manual-to-column matching; it provides the larger target-localization anchor, though three tag-name discrepancies and reused events across seeds remain caveats. RAID (Sensors 2026) directly overlaps the HAI dataset and explanation pipeline, but its 87% result evaluates language propositions against the detector's own Isolation Forest decision paths, not external attack-target labels. Our normal-only 500-tree Isolation Forest reference detected 22.2/29 events on average and achieved unconditional grouped-deletion Hit@5 0.297; it exceeded the conditional random-rank expectation of 0.083, but was not distinguishable from PCA-TEMPORAL grouped ablation (paired difference -0.014, 95% event-bootstrap CI [-0.214, 0.186], Holm-adjusted $p=0.945$). It uses 79 raw tags plus differences and is not RAID's 330-feature regime-aware model. Data4Cyber v3 adds a separate smart-grid testbed with row-level phase/event logs and endpoint/register maps, but its checksum-verified 14,354 rows collapse to only a handful of target-eligible attack events; GRIDGUARD-AI also overlaps this dataset and scenario-held-out assurance. It should test evidence-resolution reporting and transfer, not carry confirmatory target statistics alone. BATADAL offers only five approximate component events and remains descriptive. Analyze each dataset separately, use unconditional first-alarm localization with misses counted as misses, report evidence coverage/abstention, and compare with matched random and native-attribution baselines. This remains a bounded evaluation candidate with reduced novelty confidence, not a new explainer or causal model. No dataset in the current set has yet been shown to support a validated process model or safety constraint.

### Updated feasibility evidence and novelty boundary (31 October 2026)

The identity-consistent five-seed rerun added a normal-only ridge one-step transition residual screen to capability/support recourse. It reduced detected-attack pass rates from 0.947 to 0.544 for PCA-STATE, 0.614 to 0.345 for PCA-TEMPORAL, and 0.222 to 0.067 for RobustZ-TEMPORAL. Sampled false-alarm pass rates were 0.898, 0.010, and 0.522, respectively, showing strongly model-dependent behavior. For PCA-TEMPORAL, successful-candidate exact-target intersection was 0.275 with support-only recourse and 0.385 with the screen, but unconditional target hits were 5/29 per seed and 4–5/29, respectively; the paired difference CI included zero and the test found no improvement. These findings do not validate the process-plausibility claim; they show that a generic normal-data transition predictor is insufficient as a physical constraint and that recourse success alone is not target explanation. AR-Pro's direct HAI counterfactual-repair study further narrows the novelty boundary. The process-constrained counterfactual remains a high-impact *conditional* method direction, but current HAI/BATADAL/Data4Cyber evidence has not established a validated process model. The new cross-layer explanation candidate is also unvalidated: it must pass the Data4Cyber schema, leakage, and scenario-count gates before it can replace the existing HAI/BATADAL audit draft.

The official HAI 21.03 paper's Appendix A supplies a usable post-hoc action taxonomy (SP, PV, CV) for scenarios AP01–AP25, which we have now transcribed and used to stratify exact-target outcomes without exposing scenario labels to the explainer. Across the 29 complete exact-target events, PCA-TEMPORAL native hit@5 ranged from 0.000 in SP-only events (n=7) to 0.750 in PV+SP events (n=4); CV-only events (n=9) scored 0.556. These strata are small and descriptive; this is a new evaluation axis, not a validated method contribution. A separate evidence audit found that HAI's published setpoint schedule and scenario descriptions do not provide an executable, calibrated process model for this study. For example, the paper identifies P1_B2016 as an SP tag but the raw train1 range (0.9473–2.0103) cannot be compared directly with the paper's pressure schedule values (0.03–0.1) without a documented scale/unit conversion. Thus HAI supports action labels and target auditing, but not the current paper's physical-feasibility constraint claim. See `docs/research/PROCESS_CONSTRAINT_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md` and `results/summaries/hai21_identity_consistent_target_localization_by_action_seed42_46.json`.

A corrected factorial audit crosses perturbation semantics with ablation semantics. At 2% jitter, PCA-TEMPORAL top-five Jaccard was 0.989 for legacy ablation with independent state/difference noise, 0.717 for the same legacy ablation with identity-preserving input jitter, and 0.701 for identity-preserving ablation under either input design (35 repeated attack IDs). With ablation semantics fixed to legacy, independent-minus-identity-preserving input difference was +0.272 (95% CI [0.194, 0.350], Holm-adjusted sign-flip $p=0.0018$); with identity-preserving ablation fixed, the difference was zero. The difference-in-differences was +0.272 (95% CI [0.197, 0.351], adjusted $p=0.0018$). Thus the observed effect is an interaction, not evidence that input validity alone determines stability. At 5% coherent jitter, identity-preserving ablation stability was 0.558. This is a local measurement-protocol finding using synthetic jitter, not a robustness contribution: it does not test adaptive attacks or operating-regime shifts. The earlier own-baseline comparison and 0.717/0.535 estimate are superseded by the crossed analysis.

For JoCE, the publisher's article page and the SNU SLCF Lab publication listing were checked for a public implementation; no author code link or reproducible repository was verified in those sources during this review. This is a bounded search result, not proof that code is unavailable elsewhere. The present HAI detectors score the current state or current state plus its first difference; they do not consume a multi-step temporal window. A JoCE comparison would therefore require either a verified faithful implementation adapted to the detector's input semantics or a new temporal-window detector and matched protocol. Do not label a temporal attribution or the current support-only search as a JoCE reproduction.

## Provisional research design

### Candidate research questions

- **RQ1:** Do attacker-capability and process constraints produce more plausible, temporally coherent counterfactual explanations than unconstrained time-series counterfactuals and attribution baselines, without materially reducing explanation fidelity?
- **RQ2:** How accurately do explanations identify the documented attacked component and relevant interval across sensor attacks, actuator attacks, and multi-stage attacks?
- **RQ3:** How do alarm timing, attack magnitude, categorical actuators, and process regime affect explanation quality and stability?
- **RQ4:** Can explanation generation run asynchronously within an explicit operational budget without delaying the detector or control loop?

### Falsifiable hypotheses (to test, not established)

- **H1:** Constrained counterfactuals reduce violations of normal-process constraints and improve attack-target localization relative to unconstrained counterfactual baselines, while retaining a pre-registered minimum sufficiency/fidelity rate.
- **H2:** Explanation quality varies with time from attack onset to alarm and differs between continuous sensors and discrete actuators; reporting peak-only explanations hides these differences.
- **H3:** A cached/lightweight feasibility model and asynchronous explanation service keep detector latency unchanged and bound explanation p95 latency/memory on the selected edge hardware. Numerical budgets must be set from the chosen plant/application requirements, not invented in advance.

### Threat model and boundaries

Initial scope: an attacker can alter a subset of observable process tags (sensor readings and/or actuator commands) over a bounded interval, with capability sets declared per scenario; the detector observes only the features available at inference. The attacker may know the detector family but does not modify the explanation service, training data, plant firmware, or the physical process model. Evaluate both white-box and score-query settings only if the chosen explainer supports them. Do not imply coverage of network-layer attacks when only historian/process tags are evaluated. Separate attacks with known component targets from attacks with only coarse time labels.

### Architecture candidate

1. **Data and provenance layer:** dataset adapters retain timestamps, train/validation/test period, attack IDs, channel types, and any documented attack-target metadata. Never infer a target label from the model’s attribution.
2. **Detector layer:** a small set of reproducible, strong process-level baselines (e.g., prediction/reconstruction detector, GDN/GRN-style detector, and a simple classical baseline). Detector outputs anomaly score and per-channel residuals where available.
3. **Explanation layer:** a candidate counterfactual optimizer searches over sparse time-channel edits allowed by the scenario’s attacker capability set. It minimizes the anomaly score subject to alert-flip sufficiency, temporal regularity, channel bounds/types, and a process-plausibility constraint estimated only from training-normal data or supplied engineering invariants.
4. **Confidence/abstention:** report feasibility, achieved score reduction, constraint residuals, and whether multiple distinct counterfactuals fit. Abstain from naming a single root cause where evidence is non-identifiable.
5. **Evaluation layer:** compare to SHAP/Integrated Gradients or gradients, raw residual ranking, ShaTS, and JoCE where implementations and licenses permit. Measure detector quality, attack-target localization, sufficiency/fidelity, sparsity, temporal coherence, process plausibility, stability under benign perturbations, explanation latency, p95 latency, and memory/CPU/GPU cost.

A minimal mathematical form to refine after dataset/algorithm choices is:

\[
\min_{\Delta,\,M}\quad \lambda_0\|M\|_0 + \lambda_1\|\Delta\|_{W,1} + \lambda_2\,\mathrm{TV}(\Delta)
 + \lambda_3\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{proc}}(X+\Delta)
\]

subject to \(M\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{adv}}\), \(\mathrm{type}(X+\Delta)=\mathrm{type}(X)\), channel bounds and discrete-state constraints, and \(s(X+\Delta)\le\tau-\kappa\). Here \(X\) is the alerted window, \(s\) the frozen detector score, \(\tau\) its validation-only threshold, \(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{adv}}\) the declared attacker-capable channel set, and \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{proc}}\) a normal-process consistency penalty. This is an optimization proposal, not a validated method. A high-fidelity counterfactual is not automatically a causal explanation or a safe operator action. The publisher abstract for XAI-CPS reports 92.9% mean F1, while the supplied PDF conclusion reports 94.7%; the conflicting values remain unresolved and neither should be used for comparative claims until the version/calculation discrepancy is reconciled.

### Experimental integrity requirements

- Split by time and complete attack scenario, never by overlapping windows at random. Fit scalers, normality models, thresholds, and feature/group definitions on training/validation only.
- Report event-level and point-level performance; avoid relying on point-adjusted F1 alone. Include per-attack and per-channel results and false alarms per unit time.
- Calibrate thresholds only on a held-out normal validation segment; disclose any test-label use in attack-target scoring.
- Define localization ground truth from attack logs/documented scenario descriptions before examining explanations. Report ambiguous/unlabeled cases separately.
- Run repeated seeds where stochastic, paired bootstrap or suitable paired tests at the event level, confidence intervals, and a pre-specified multiple-comparison correction.
- Time detector and explanation separately on disclosed hardware; report p50/p95 and asynchronous queueing behavior. No inference that a one-second or other deadline is universal.
- Keep licensed iTrust SWaT/WaDi files out of Git. The official iTrust pages describe dataset request/access mechanisms; configure adapters for user-provided copies and document exact version/checksums without redistributing raw files.

## Dataset plan (provisional)

Prioritize datasets with time-stamped process channels and attack windows, not generic flow-only IIoT data. HAI 21.03 provides the primary experiment candidate: its maintainers publish attack records with controller, action, and target-point metadata, though 21 exact tag lists remain unresolved and no independently validated numeric plant model is supplied. BATADAL is a second, publicly downloadable water-network simulation candidate: Dataset04 has approximate positive labels plus unknown rows, and the official FAQ identifies pump-component targets for five approximate intervals. These support a small cross-dataset target-localization audit, not a fully labeled second benchmark or exact physical replay; its published EPANET demand patterns differ from those used to generate telemetry. SWaT and WADI are relevant extensions but require iTrust access and data-use terms; include them only with authorized local copies. For a process-transition-valid counterfactual method, acquire a dataset whose version-matched numeric model and operating inputs are available and independently validated before claiming physical feasibility.

## Immediate reviewer risks

1. **Novelty collision:** JoCE already does temporal counterfactual explanations and includes SWaT; XAI-CPS already claims physics-aware explanations; NDSS already benchmarks attribution against manipulated features. The proposed work must show the attacker-action constraints and operational validity add measurable value beyond these.
2. **Dataset leakage and metric inflation:** overlapping windows, preprocessing on the full sequence, attack identity leakage, and point adjustment can produce optimistic IDS results.
3. **False actionability:** a score-flipping input edit may be physically impossible, may correspond to an attack that never occurred, and must not be presented as a repair instruction.
4. **Weak ground truth:** attack time labels do not necessarily identify the root sensor/actuator. Distinguish event detection, affected-process localization, and adversary-target attribution.
5. **Scope creep:** the paper should not also introduce federated learning, reinforcement learning response, protocol inspection, and a new detector unless an experiment shows each is essential.
