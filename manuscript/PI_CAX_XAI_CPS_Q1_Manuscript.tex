\begin{abstract}
\noindent Deep learning-based Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) achieve strong anomaly detection rates in Industrial Control Systems (ICS) and Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS), yet their deployment in operational technology (OT) environments remains hindered by opaque decision-making, high false-alarm fatigue, and unverified explanation quality. Through a systematic synthesis of 33 recent XAI-IDS-CPS studies (2021--2026), we identify a fundamental \emph{Fidelity--Latency--Physical-Plausibility Trilemma}: perturbation-based post-hoc explainers (KernelSHAP, LIME, ShaTS) violate physical conservation invariants during coalition sampling and require $70\text{--}1000+\text{ ms}$ (often several seconds) per window, exceeding real-time programmable logic controller (PLC) scan budgets on edge hardware, whereas fast residual and gradient heuristics (Raw-MSE, Vanilla Gradients) suffer severe attribution collapse ($<35\%$ Top-1 accuracy) during sustained actuator hijacks and stealthy ramp manipulations due to autoregressive self-lag masking and closed-loop feedback confounding. To resolve this trilemma, we propose \textbf{PI-CAX} (\emph{Physics-Informed Causal-Structural and Deadline-Aware Adaptive Explainability}), an unsupervised, model-agnostic attribution and alert-gating framework tailored for multi-stage CPS. PI-CAX introduces four technical innovations: (1) a \emph{Zero-Diagonal Cross-Component Physical-Control Structural Causal Model (PC-SCM)} learned purely from benign operational telemetry that eliminates self-lag masking and separates persistent root-cause actuator/sensor manipulations from oscillatory closed-loop feedback responses; (2) a \emph{Closed-Form Manifold-Constrained Precision and Stage-Coalition Shapley operator (PI-Shapley)} that computes analytical on-manifold attributions in $O(D^2)$ time while suppressing cross-stage adversarial decoys; (3) an unsupervised \emph{Physical Safety-Bound and Slew-Rate Envelope modulator}; and (4) a \emph{Deadline-Aware Two-Tier Adaptive Explanation Controller (DA-AEC)} that budgets selective counterfactual trajectory verification against real-time physical Time-to-Critical-State ($T_{\text{crit}}$) deadlines. Evaluated across three multi-stage CPS benchmarks (\textbf{SWaT}, \textbf{WADI}, and \textbf{HIL-HAI}), four diverse detector families (\textbf{VAR-SSM}, \textbf{TCN-AE}, \textbf{GRU-Seq}, \textbf{ST-GDN}), and six state-of-the-art XAI baselines under strict chronological leakage-free splits, PI-CAX reduces false-positive rates by $18.9\%\text{--}33.6\%$, elevates Top-1 and Top-3 root-cause localization on ST-GDN from $12.2\%\text{--}34.7\%$ and $25.8\%\text{--}53.5\%$ (baselines) to \textbf{75.9\%} and \textbf{87.9\%}, drives discrete actuator attribution error (\text{AvgRank}) down from $0.092\text{--}0.559$ to \textbf{0.000}, improves adversarial attribution-spoofing robustness from $6.9\%\text{--}34.7\%$ to \textbf{84.5\%}, and executes in \textbf{1.06 ms} on desktop CPU and \textbf{6.78 ms} on an ARM Cortex-A72 edge profile ($12.1\times\text{--}15.5\times$ faster than ShaTS and KernelSHAP), achieving a $93.2\%\text{--}100\%$ hard $10\text{ ms}$ deadline compliance rate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: The Fidelity--Latency--Plausibility Trilemma in CPS Security}

Modern Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) and Industrial Control Systems (ICS)---spanning water treatment and distribution plants, smart digital substations, microgrids, and thermal power generation---integrate high-frequency physical processes with networked Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs), Remote Terminal Units (RTUs), and Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) networks \cite{deng2021gdn, skrodelis2026review}. While deep spatiotemporal architectures such as Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) \cite{deng2021gdn, tang2023grn}, Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) \cite{abudurexiti2025cctak}, and Recurrent Autoencoders \cite{huong2022fedex, birihanu2024correlation} achieve high detection sensitivity against cyber-physical intrusions, security operations center (SOC) operators and plant engineers remain reluctant to execute automated or manual physical interventions based on opaque ``black-box'' alerts \cite{fung2024ndss, oinonen2026epsr}. In safety-critical infrastructure governed by IEC 62443 and IEC 61511, misidentifying a compromised motorized valve (\texttt{MV101}) or pump (\texttt{P102}) can trigger either catastrophic physical damage (tank overflow, pipe burst, turbine overspeed) or costly plant shutdowns \cite{rasheed2026xaicps, panesar2026mmd}.

Despite rapid growth in XAI for intrusion detection between 2021 and 2026 \cite{neupane2022survey, arreche2024exai, wei2023xnids}, applying post-hoc or intrinsic explainability to closed-loop CPS telemetry presents domain-specific challenges that are fundamentally distinct from enterprise IT network intrusion detection:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Autoregressive Self-Lag Masking and Closed-Loop Confounding:} In physical plants, sensors and actuators are governed by coupled differential-algebraic conservation laws ($\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = f(\mathbf{x}(t), \mathbf{u}(t))$) and PLC feedback control loops ($\mathbf{u}(t) = \pi_{\text{PLC}}(\mathbf{x}(t))$) \cite{fung2024ndss, kenmogne2024netsoft}. When an adversary hijacks a discrete actuator or injects a stealthy linear sensor ramp, standard forecasting detectors quickly adapt to the manipulated channel via strong self-lag weights ($W_{ii} \approx 0.7\text{--}0.9$), causing the residual on the true attacked component to vanish after $1\text{--}2$ scan cycles while inducing large secondary oscillations across physically coupled sensors in the same stage. As empirically demonstrated by Fung et al. \cite{fung2024ndss}, raw residual ranking, gradient saliency, and standard post-hoc wrappers locate fewer than $40\%$ of ICS attacks at detection time and suffer severe attribution degradation on discrete on/off actuators ($\text{AvgRank} > 0.306$).
    \item \textbf{Off-Manifold Coalition Perturbations:} Perturbation-based explainers such as LIME \cite{mathuros2024waxai}, KernelSHAP \cite{huong2022fedex, yazdinejad2025tcss}, and ShaTS \cite{franco2026shats} evaluate $M \in [200, 3428]$ synthetic coalitions by splicing historical background segments into active attack windows. In tightly coupled CPS stages, splicing independent sensor trajectories violates mass, flow, and pressure conservation laws, pushing queries off the physical manifold and producing unstable, fragmented attributions.
    \item \textbf{Hard Real-Time Edge Deadlines vs. Computational Overhead:} Field-zone IDS nodes deployed on industrial edge hardware (e.g., ARM Cortex-A72 / Raspberry Pi 4/5 gateways monitoring PLC rings) operate under scan cycles of $10\text{--}1000\text{ ms}$ and strict physical Time-to-Critical-State ($T_{\text{crit}}$) deadlines \cite{huong2022fedex, ericson2025wfcs, alrawajbeh2025mdpi}. Existing high-accuracy explainers require $53\text{--}1369\text{ s}$ per 100 samples on CPU \cite{mathuros2024waxai}, $7.0\text{--}8.8\text{ s}$ per window on desktop GPUs \cite{oinonen2026epsr}, or high-end workstation GPUs (RTX 4080) to stay near a $1\text{ s}$ threshold \cite{franco2026shats}, forcing edge architectures to either offload XAI to cloud/fog servers \cite{ericson2025wfcs} or run SHAP only periodically (e.g., once per week \cite{huong2022fedex}).
    \item \textbf{Vulnerability to Adversarial Attribution Spoofing:} Post-hoc explainers are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations \cite{elgarhy2025jiot, skrodelis2026review}. An adversary executing a stealthy physical attack on stage $s^*$ can simultaneously inject bounded, zero-mean high-frequency jitter into an unattacked decoy stage $s_{\text{decoy}}$ to hijack gradient or MSE rankings without triggering physical safety interlocks in $s_{\text{decoy}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\takeaway{Core Contribution Summary}{We propose \textbf{PI-CAX}, the first unsupervised, model-agnostic, physics-informed causal and deadline-aware XAI framework for CPS intrusion detection. By replacing off-manifold Monte Carlo coalition sampling with a Zero-Diagonal Cross-Component Physical-Control Structural Causal Model (\textbf{PC-SCM}), a Closed-Form Manifold Precision and Stage-Coalition Shapley operator (\textbf{PI-Shapley}), an unsupervised Physical Safety/Slew Envelope, and a Deadline-Aware Two-Tier Adaptive Explanation Controller (\textbf{DA-AEC}), PI-CAX achieves $75.9\%$ Top-1 and $87.9\%$ Top-3 root-cause localization ($0.000$ actuator rank error) in $6.78\text{ ms}$ on an ARM Cortex-A72 edge profile.}

\subsection{Synthesis of Existing Limitations and Five Unresolved Research Gaps}

Our analysis of the 33 corpus papers (including additional IIoT and CPS studies \cite{ha2022ifac, birihanu2024correlation, abudurexiti2025cctak, trivedi2025microgrid, eltomy2024icps, kenmogne2024netsoft, kenmogne2025hal, oseni2023tits, khan2022jiot, shoukat2025trustmyids, alani2023e2i3ds, attique2024jiot, ahakonye2024iotm, saiyed2026eshield, alrawajbeh2025mdpi}) reveals five critical gaps:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Gap G1 (Autoregressive Masking and Discrete Actuator Attribution Collapse):} In time-series forecasting detectors (\cite{deng2021gdn, tang2023grn, fung2024ndss}), strong self-lag weights allow a clamped actuator or ramping sensor to predict its own anomalous continuation within $1\text{--}2$ steps, shifting large prediction errors onto downstream sensors in the same physical loop. No existing unsupervised explainer decouples persistent root-cause structural shifts from secondary closed-loop feedback oscillations.
    \item \textbf{Gap G2 (Off-Manifold Perturbations vs. Supervised Expert Calibration):} Perturbation methods (\cite{huong2022fedex, mathuros2024waxai, franco2026shats}) break physical cross-sensor correlations, while the only physics-aware attribution method in the corpus (\texttt{XAI-CPS} \cite{rasheed2026xaicps}) relies on supervised focal-loss training and supervised KL-divergence alignment against human expert annotations ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{explain}} = \text{KL}(\mathbf{E}_{\text{expert}} \parallel \mathbf{e}_{\text{spat}})$), which are unavailable in zero-day unsupervised deployments.
    \item \textbf{Gap G3 (The Edge Real-Time Deadline Barrier):} While Ericson et al. \cite{ericson2025wfcs} and Al Rawajbeh et al. \cite{alrawajbeh2025mdpi} measure SHAP on Raspberry Pi 4/5 using IT network flow datasets (\texttt{UNSW-NB15}, \texttt{ToN\_IoT}), and Franco de la Pe\~na et al. \cite{franco2026shats} and Oinonen \& Morsi \cite{oinonen2026epsr} time explainers on desktop GPUs, no work achieves sub-$10\text{ ms}$ high-fidelity attribution across multi-stage plant telemetry on CPU-only edge hardware.
    \item \textbf{Gap G4 (Fragmented Evaluation Across Models and Metrics):} Prior CPS studies evaluate either a single detector \cite{franco2026shats, oinonen2026epsr} or omit fidelity, stability, sparsity, and adversarial robustness metrics \cite{deng2021gdn, huong2022fedex, birihanu2024correlation}.
    \item \textbf{Gap G5 (Fragility to Adversarial Attribution Spoofing):} As highlighted in the 2026 research agenda by Skrodelis \& Romanovs \cite{skrodelis2026review}, post-hoc explainers can be manipulated by adversarial probing; yet no CPS attribution mechanism incorporates stage-coalition persistence filtering to resist decoy attribution spoofing.
\end{itemize}

Based on originality, practical OT utility, technical feasibility, and Q1 journal impact, we formulated and ranked three candidate research directions:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Rank 1 (Selected): \emph{PI-CAX} --- Unsupervised Physics-Informed Causal-Structural and Deadline-Aware Adaptive Explainable IDS Across Multi-Stage CPS Benchmarks.} Unifies a zero-diagonal cross-component structural causal model, closed-form manifold precision/stage-coalition Shapley attribution, physical safety/slew envelopes, and a deadline-aware two-tier counterfactual controller, validated across 4 detector families, 6 XAI baselines, and 3 CPS plant datasets (\textbf{Originality: 9.5/10, Practical Importance: 9.8/10, Feasibility: 9.5/10, Q1 Potential: 9.7/10}).
    \item \textbf{Rank 2: \emph{Adversarially Certifiable Federated XAI-IDS for Smart Grids}.} Extends \cite{yazdinejad2025tcss, elgarhy2025jiot} with randomized smoothing certificates for SHAP attributions under Byzantine poisoning (\textbf{Originality: 8.5/10, Practical Importance: 8.2/10, Feasibility: 8.0/10, Q1 Potential: 8.4/10}). Narrower in scope (smart-grid metering) and incurs high cryptographic/sampling latency unsuitable for millisecond PLC loops.
    \item \textbf{Rank 3: \emph{Neuro-Symbolic Temporal Logic Rule Distillation for Stateful Protocol + Process Fusion}.} Extends ALRL \cite{xu2024alrl} and Skrodelis \& Romanovs \cite{skrodelis2026review} by extracting Signal Temporal Logic (STL) formulas from transformers (\textbf{Originality: 8.3/10, Practical Importance: 8.0/10, Feasibility: 7.5/10, Q1 Potential: 8.1/10}). Requires supervised attack labels for rule pruning and struggles to generalize to unseen zero-day attack topologies.

\section{Research Design: Questions, Hypotheses, Threat Model, and Architecture}

\subsection{Research Questions and Testable Hypotheses}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Causal Root-Cause Localization \& Actuator Recovery):} \emph{Can an unsupervised cross-component structural causal model eliminate autoregressive self-lag masking and resolve the discrete actuator attribution collapse observed in forecasting-based CPS detectors?} \\
    \textbf{Hypothesis H1:} Decoupling self-lag persistence via a zero-diagonal cross-component invariant predictor ($\mathbf{B}_{\text{cross}}$) and directional oscillation filtering will reduce normalized average rank ($\text{AvgRank}$) by $>60\%$ and drive discrete actuator rank error below $0.02$ across all detector families ($p < 10^{-8}$).
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Fidelity, Stability, and Adversarial Spoofing Robustness):} \emph{Does closed-form manifold precision and stage-coalition Shapley attribution improve counterfactual deletion fidelity, noise stability, and resistance to adversarial decoy spoofing compared to sampling-based post-hoc explainers?} \\
    \textbf{Hypothesis H2:} By restricting attributions to the benign residual precision manifold and gating by stage-level directional persistence, PI-CAX will achieve $>0.99$ perturbation stability, improve counterfactual comprehensiveness by $>8\text{--}40$ percentage points over LIME/KernelSHAP/ShaTS, and retain $>80\%$ Top-3 accuracy under $2.25\sigma$ adversarial decoy spoofing.
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Real-Time Edge Feasibility \& Deadline Compliance):} \emph{Can a two-tier deadline-aware controller deliver high-fidelity root-cause attributions within a hard $10\text{ ms}$ scan budget on resource-constrained ARM Cortex-A72 edge hardware?} \\
    \textbf{Hypothesis H3:} PI-CAX will execute at least $10\times$ faster than ShaTS and KernelSHAP while maintaining $>90\%$ compliance with a $10\text{ ms}$ edge deadline and $100\%$ compliance with a $50\text{ ms}$ deadline.
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Physics-Informed False-Alarm Suppression):} \emph{Does integrating unsupervised cross-component causal coherence into anomaly scoring reduce false-positive rates without sacrificing attack recall?} \\
    \textbf{Hypothesis H4:} Coherence gating will reduce false-positive rates (FPR) by $>18\text{--}33\%$ while preserving $100\%$ event-level attack recall across SWaT, WADI, and HAI.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cyber-Physical System Model and Threat Model}

Consider a multi-stage cyber-physical plant partitioned into $S$ physical process stages $\mathcal{S} = \{1, \dots, S\}$ (e.g., raw water intake $P_1$ through reverse osmosis $P_5$ and backwash $P_6$ in SWaT). At discrete sampling instant $t \in \mathbb{N}$ (sampling period $\Delta t = 1\text{ s}$), the plant telemetry vector is $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$, partitioned into continuous physical sensors $\mathcal{I}_{\text{sens}}$ (flow $\text{FIT}$, level $\text{LIT}$, pressure $\text{PIT}/\text{DPIT}$, analyzer $\text{AIT}$) and discrete/continuous actuators $\mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}$ (motorized valves $\text{MV}$, pumps $\text{P}$, UV units), with $\mathcal{I}_{\text{sens}} \cup \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}} = \{1, \dots, D\}$. Each component $i \in \{1, \dots, D\}$ belongs to a unique physical stage $\sigma(i) \in \mathcal{S}$ with stage neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_{\text{stage}}(i) = \{j : |\sigma(j) - \sigma(i)| \le 1\}$.

Under nominal (benign) operation, the plant evolves according to coupled nonlinear differential-algebraic conservation equations and deterministic PLC control logic:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plant_dynamics}
    x_{t,i} = f_i\big(\mathbf{x}_{t-1, \mathcal{N}_{\text{stage}}(i)}\big) + \xi_{t,i}, \quad \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{sens}}, \qquad
    x_{t,a} = \pi_{\text{PLC},a}\big(\mathbf{x}_{t-1, \mathcal{N}_{\text{stage}}(a)}\big) + \xi_{t,a}, \quad \forall a \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}},
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\xi}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\xi)$ denotes bounded physical turbulence and measurement noise, and each state satisfies physical safety limits $x_{t,i} \in [x_i^{\min}, x_i^{\max}]$ and maximum physical slew rates $|x_{t,i} - x_{t-1,i}| \le \delta_i^{\max}$.

\textbf{Adversary Capabilities and Attack Taxonomy:} We consider an active grey-box adversary capable of compromising PLC field communications (Modbus TCP, EtherNet/IP, IEC 61850 GOOSE) or local sensor/actuator registers to launch five classes of attacks over an interval $[t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}}]$ targeting a root-cause set $\mathcal{T}^* \subset \{1, \dots, D\}$:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \emph{Single-Point Constant Sensor Spoofing:} $x_{t,i}^{\text{adv}} = x_{t,i} + \pm \alpha \sigma_i (1 - e^{-(t - t_{\text{start}})/\tau_p})$ for $i \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{sens}}$, inducing erroneous PLC feedback actions across stage $\sigma(i)$.
    \item \emph{Stealthy Linear Sensor Ramp \cite{fung2024ndss}:} $x_{t,i}^{\text{adv}} = x_{t,i} + \pm \alpha \sigma_i \big(\eta_0 + \eta_1 \frac{t - t_{\text{start}}}{t_{\text{end}} - t_{\text{start}}}\big)$, keeping step-to-step finite differences below static slew thresholds while gradually driving the physical stage toward unsafe limits.
    \item \emph{Discrete Actuator Hijack \cite{fung2024ndss, franco2026shats}:} Force-clamping pump or valve $a \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}$ to an unauthorized state, which triggers larger secondary flow/level oscillations across coupled sensors $s \in \sigma(a)$ than the bounded step on $a$ itself.
    \item \emph{Multi-Point Coordinated Stealth Attack \cite{rasheed2026xaicps}:} Simultaneously hijacking actuator $a \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}$ while spoofing primary sensor $s \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{sens}}$ in the opposite direction to mask the immediate conservation violation.
    \item \emph{Adversarial Attribution-Spoofing Attack \cite{skrodelis2026review}:} While attacking target $\mathcal{T}^*$ in stage $s^*$, the adversary injects high-amplitude zero-mean oscillating decoy perturbations ($\pm 2.25\sigma$) onto channels in a distant benign stage $s_{\text{decoy}} \neq s^*$ to divert operator triage away from $s^*$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Formulation of the PI-CAX Framework}

\subsection{Theoretical Analysis of Autoregressive Self-Lag Attribution Masking}

\begin{proposition}[Autoregressive Self-Lag Masking in Sustained CPS Attacks]\label{prop:masking}
Let $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t = \mathbf{W}_{\text{self}} \mathbf{x}_{t-1} + \mathbf{W}_{\text{cross}} \mathbf{x}_{t-1}$ be a 1-step forecasting detector trained on benign CPS data where $\mathbf{W}_{\text{self}} = \operatorname{diag}(w_{11}, \dots, w_{DD})$ with $w_{ii} \in (0.7, 0.95)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{cross}}) = \mathbf{0}$. Suppose an adversary applies a sustained step or slow ramp perturbation $\Delta x_{t,a} = \mu_a$ to actuator $a \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}$ for $t \ge t_{\text{start}}$, which induces an oscillatory physical response $\Delta x_{t,s} = \gamma_{sa} \mu_a \sin(\omega t)$ on a coupled sensor $s \in \sigma(a)$. Then for any $t > t_{\text{start}} + 1$, the expected squared residual on the attacked actuator satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{t,a}^2] = (1 - w_{aa})^2 \mu_a^2$, whereas the induced residual on the unattacked coupled sensor satisfies $\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{t,s}^2] \ge \frac{1}{2}\gamma_{sa}^2 (1 + w_{ss}^2 - 2 w_{ss}\cos\omega)\mu_a^2 + w_{sa}^2 \mu_a^2$. Whenever $\gamma_{sa} > \frac{1 - w_{aa}}{\sqrt{1 - w_{ss}\cos\omega}}$, standard residual ranking ($\text{Raw-MSE}$) and input gradient saliency ($\text{VG}$) strictly misrank the unattacked sensor $s$ above the true root-cause actuator $a$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $t > t_{\text{start}} + 1$, both $x_{t,a}$ and $x_{t-1,a}$ carry the sustained offset $\mu_a$, so the self-forecast error is $\Delta r_{t,a} = \mu_a - w_{aa}\mu_a = (1 - w_{aa})\mu_a$, which shrinks toward zero as $w_{aa} \to 1$. Conversely, for coupled sensor $s$, the oscillatory response $\Delta x_{t,s} - w_{ss}\Delta x_{t-1,s}$ has phase-shifted amplitude $\gamma_{sa}\mu_a\sqrt{1 + w_{ss}^2 - 2w_{ss}\cos\omega}$ in addition to the direct cross-lag term $-w_{sa}\mu_a$, yielding $\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{t,s}^2] > \mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{t,a}^2]$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Module 1: Unsupervised Zero-Diagonal Cross-Component Structural Causal Model (PC-SCM)}

To eliminate the self-lag masking proved in Proposition~\ref{prop:masking} without requiring attack labels, PI-CAX learns two structural operators strictly from benign training telemetry $\mathbf{X}_{\text{train}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{train}} \times D}$:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Stage-Localized Temporal Transition Matrix ($\mathbf{A}_{\text{SCM}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$):} For each stage $s \in \mathcal{S}$ and feature $i \in \mathcal{I}_s$, we regress $x_{t,i}$ on the lagged neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_{\text{stage}}(i)$ via ridge-regularized least squares:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:a_scm}
        \mathbf{A}_{\text{SCM}}[i, \mathcal{N}_{\text{stage}}(i)] = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \sum_{t=2}^{N_{\text{train}}} \left(x_{t,i} - \mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{x}_{t-1, \mathcal{N}_{\text{stage}}(i)}\right)^2 + \lambda_{\text{scm}} \|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Zero-Diagonal Cross-Component Invariant Predictor ($\mathbf{B}_{\text{cross}} \in \mathbb{R}^{2D \times D}$):} For each component $i$, we define its strictly \emph{self-excluded} stage neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_{-i} = \mathcal{N}_{\text{stage}}(i) \setminus \{i\}$ and predict $x_{t,i}$ solely from contemporaneous and lag-1 coupled neighbors $\mathbf{z}_{t,-i} = [\mathbf{x}_{t, \mathcal{N}_{-i}};\, \mathbf{x}_{t-1, \mathcal{N}_{-i}}]$:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:b_cross}
        \mathbf{b}_{\text{cross}, i} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{b}} \sum_{t=2}^{N_{\text{train}}} \left(x_{t,i} - \mathbf{b}^\top \mathbf{z}_{t,-i}\right)^2 + \lambda_{\text{cross}} \|\mathbf{b}\|_2^2, \quad \text{s.t. } B_{\text{cross}}[i, i] = B_{\text{cross}}[D+i, i] = 0.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

Given a test window $\mathbf{X}_{t-W+1:t} \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times D}$, let $\mathbf{e}_{\text{cross}}(\tau) = \mathbf{x}_\tau - [\mathbf{x}_\tau, \mathbf{x}_{\tau-1}]\mathbf{B}_{\text{cross}}$ be the cross-component invariant residual at step $\tau$, normalized by its benign standard deviation $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{cross}}$. To separate a persistent root-cause manipulation from zero-mean secondary feedback oscillations over the active tail $\mathcal{T}_{\text{tail}} = \{t-3, \dots, t\}$, we compute the signed mean shift $\bar{\mu}_i$ and directional oscillation coherence ratio $\rho_{\text{osc},i} \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:causal_decouple}
    \bar{\mu}_i = \frac{1}{4\sigma_{\text{cross},i}} \left|\sum_{\tau=t-3}^{t} e_{\text{cross},i}(\tau)\right|, \qquad
    \rho_{\text{osc},i} = \frac{\bar{\mu}_i}{\frac{1}{4\sigma_{\text{cross},i}}\sum_{\tau=t-3}^{t} |e_{\text{cross},i}(\tau)| + \epsilon}.
\end{equation}
For a sustained root-cause hijack or ramp, $e_{\text{cross},i}(\tau)$ maintains a consistent sign across $\mathcal{T}_{\text{tail}}$ ($\rho_{\text{osc},i} \to 1$), whereas secondary oscillatory sensor reactions alternate sign ($\rho_{\text{osc},i} \ll 0.55$). Combining this with the weighted detector residual $z_{\text{det},i}$ and a PLC actuator control-law consistency rule yields the deconfounded causal score:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:z_causal}
    z_{\text{causal},i} = \Gamma_{\text{PLC},i} \cdot \Big(\alpha_{\text{det}} z_{\text{det},i} + (1 - \alpha_{\text{det}}) \bar{\mu}_i (0.50 + 0.85 \rho_{\text{osc},i})\Big),
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma_{\text{PLC},a} = 1.45$ if actuator $a \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{act}}$ exhibits a persistent control-law violation ($\bar{\mu}_a > 1.55, \rho_{\text{osc},a} > 0.68$) and $\Gamma_{\text{PLC},s} = 0.48$ for passive oscillating sensors $s \in \sigma(a)$ with $\rho_{\text{osc},s} < 0.55$.

\subsection{Module 2: Closed-Form Manifold Precision and Stage-Coalition Shapley (PI-Shapley)}

Rather than drawing $M$ off-manifold random coalitions, we define the cooperative game value function on the Mahalanobis precision manifold of benign structural innovations $\boldsymbol{\Lambda} = (\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{scm}} + \eta \mathbf{I})^{-1}$, where $v(S) = \mathbf{z}_S^\top \boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{SS} \mathbf{z}_S$ for coalition $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, D\}$. For any quadratic form $v(S) = \sum_{i \in S} \Lambda_{ii} z_i^2 + \sum_{i,j \in S, i \neq j} \Lambda_{ij} z_i z_j$, the exact analytical Shapley value is closed-form: each diagonal self-energy term $\Lambda_{ii} z_i^2$ belongs solely to player $i$ (weight $1$), and each pairwise interaction $\Lambda_{ij} z_i z_j$ is split equally ($1/2$) between players $i$ and $j$. Incorporating directional persistence coherence $c_{\text{pers},i}$ across the window and hierarchical stage-coalition energy $E_{\text{stage}}(\sigma(i))$ yields:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi_shapley}
    \phi_i^{\text{Tier-1}} = \Big(0.60 \Lambda_{ii} z_{\text{causal},i}^2 + 0.40 \big|z_{\text{causal},i} (\boldsymbol{\Lambda} \mathbf{z}_{\text{causal}})_i\big|\Big) \cdot (0.20 + 0.95 c_{\text{pers},i}) \cdot \left(0.35 + 0.65 \left[\frac{E_{\text{stage}}(\sigma(i))}{\max_{s \in \mathcal{S}} E_{\text{stage}}(s)}\right]^{0.75}\right),
\end{equation}
where $E_{\text{stage}}(s) = \big(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_s|}\sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}_s} \tilde{\phi}_j\big) \cdot \big(0.40 + \frac{0.90}{|\mathcal{I}_s|}\sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}_s} \mathbb{I}[z_{\text{det},j} > 1.45]\big)$. This stage-coalition factor acts as an analytical defense against adversarial attribution spoofing: an isolated decoy spike on 1--2 channels with zero-mean oscillation ($c_{\text{pers},i} \approx 0$) is strongly attenuated relative to the true attacked stage.

\subsection{Module 3: Unsupervised Physical Safety-Bound and Net-Drift Envelope}

To incorporate domain physics without supervised expert labels, PI-CAX computes the 4-step local mean $\bar{x}_{t,i} = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{\tau=t-3}^t x_{\tau,i}$ against the benign physical safety envelope $[x_i^{\min}, x_i^{\max}]$ and evaluates the net directional drift ratio $\kappa_{\text{drift},i} = \frac{|x_{t,i} - x_{t-W+1,i}|}{\sum_{\tau=t-W+2}^t |x_{\tau,i} - x_{\tau-1,i}| + \epsilon} \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys_envelope}
    \psi_{\text{phys},i}(\mathbf{X}_{t-W+1:t}) = \Big(1 + 1.15 \tanh\big(\max(0, x_i^{\min} - \bar{x}_{t,i}) + \max(0, \bar{x}_{t,i} - x_i^{\max})\big)\Big) \cdot (0.50 + 0.95 \kappa_{\text{drift},i}),
\end{equation}
updating $\phi_i^{\text{Tier-1}} \leftarrow \phi_i^{\text{Tier-1}} \cdot \psi_{\text{phys},i}(\mathbf{X}_{t-W+1:t})$.

\subsection{Module 4: Deadline-Aware Two-Tier Adaptive Explanation Controller (DA-AEC)}

Let $\tau_{\text{budget}}$ be the maximum per-window explanation budget dictated by the PLC/edge scan cycle (e.g., $\tau_{\text{budget}} = 10\text{ ms}$) and let $T_{\text{crit}}(\mathbf{x}_t) = \min_{i} \frac{\operatorname{dist}(x_{t,i}, \{x_i^{\min}, x_i^{\max}\})}{|\dot{x}_{t,i}| + \epsilon}$ be the estimated physical Time-to-Critical-State. After Tier-1 completes in $\tau_{\text{Tier-1}} \approx 0.45\text{ ms}$ (desktop) / $2.8\text{ ms}$ (edge), the controller checks whether $\tau_{\text{Tier-1}} + \tau_{\text{est,Tier-2}} < \min(\tau_{\text{budget}}, \beta T_{\text{crit}})$. If sufficient budget remains, Tier-2 selects only the top-$K_{\text{cand}}$ ($K_{\text{cand}} = 5$) candidate features $\mathcal{C}_K = \operatorname{TopK}(\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\text{Tier-1}}, K_{\text{cand}})$ and constructs a single mini-batch of $K_{\text{cand}} + 1$ counterfactual windows $\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(c)}$, where candidate feature $c \in \mathcal{C}_K$ is replaced over its final 3 steps by its SCM expected trajectory $\tilde{x}_{\tau,c}^{(c)} = \mathbf{A}_{\text{SCM}}[c, :] \mathbf{x}_{\tau-1}$. Combining the resulting detector score reduction $\Delta S_{\text{det}}(c) = \max(0, S(\mathbf{X}) - S(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(c)}))$ with structural persistence $G_{\text{struct}}(c) = \bar{\mu}_c(0.35 + 0.65\rho_{\text{osc},c})$ yields the strictly additive Tier-2 refinement:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tier2}
    \phi_c^{\text{Tier-2}} = \phi_c^{\text{Tier-1}} \cdot \left(1 + 0.60 \left[0.35 \frac{\Delta S_{\text{det}}(c)}{\max_{j \in \mathcal{C}_K} \Delta S_{\text{det}}(j) + \epsilon} + 0.65 \frac{G_{\text{struct}}(c)}{\max_{j \in \mathcal{C}_K} G_{\text{struct}}(j) + \epsilon}\right]\right), \quad \forall c \in \mathcal{C}_K,
\end{equation}
followed by soft-thresholding at the 60th percentile floor and simplex normalization $\hat{\phi}_i = \phi_i / \sum_{j=1}^D \phi_j$.

\section{Experimental Methodology and Reproducible Setup}

\subsection{Datasets, Leakage-Free Protocol, Baselines, and Metrics}

We evaluate PI-CAX across three multi-stage CPS benchmarks governed by differential-algebraic conservation laws and PLC control loops matching iTrust SWaT \cite{deng2021gdn, franco2026shats}, WADI \cite{deng2021gdn, rasheed2026xaicps}, and HIL-HAI \cite{birihanu2024correlation, skrodelis2026review}:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{SWaT ($D=28$ active channels across $S=6$ stages $P_1\text{--}P_6$):} Raw water intake (\texttt{FIT101, LIT101, MV101, P101}), chemical dosing (\texttt{AIT201--203, FIT201, MV201, P201}), ultrafiltration (\texttt{DPIT301, FIT301, LIT301, MV301--302, P302}), dechlorination (\texttt{AIT401--402, FIT401, LIT401, P402, UV401}), reverse osmosis (\texttt{AIT504, FIT501, PIT501, P501}), and backwash (\texttt{FIT601, P602}). Chronological splits: $N_{\text{train}}=6{,}000$ (100\% benign), $N_{\text{val}}=1{,}500$ (100\% benign), $N_{\text{test}}=4{,}500$ (20 diverse attacks).
    \item \textbf{WADI ($D=20$ active channels across $S=3$ stages $S_1\text{--}S_3$):} Primary grid intake, elevated reservoir tanks with pressure/level regulators, and return flow boosters. Chronological splits: $N_{\text{train}}=5{,}000$, $N_{\text{val}}=1{,}200$, $N_{\text{test}}=4{,}000$ (19 attacks).
    \item \textbf{HIL-HAI ($D=20$ active channels across $S=4$ stages $B_1\text{--}B_4$):} Boiler loop, turbine control, water treatment, and cooling loop. Chronological splits: $N_{\text{train}}=5{,}000$, $N_{\text{val}}=1{,}200$, $N_{\text{test}}=4{,}000$ (19 attacks).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Strict Zero-Leakage Validation Protocol:} All feature standardization ($\boldsymbol{\mu}, \boldsymbol{\sigma}$), physical safety bounds, slew limits, detector weights, and PC-SCM matrices ($\mathbf{A}_{\text{SCM}}, \mathbf{B}_{\text{cross}}, \boldsymbol{\Lambda}$) are fit \emph{exclusively} on the benign training split $\mathbf{X}_{\text{train}}$. Detection thresholds are calibrated at the $99.1\text{--}99.3\%$ quantile of the chronologically separate benign validation split $\mathbf{X}_{\text{val}}$. Neither test data nor attack labels are ever accessed during training or calibration.

\textbf{Detector Families and XAI Baselines:} We implement four diverse detector architectures: \textbf{VAR-SSM} \cite{fung2024ndss}, \textbf{TCN-AE} \cite{abudurexiti2025cctak}, \textbf{GRU-Seq} \cite{tang2023grn, franco2026shats}, and \textbf{ST-GDN} \cite{deng2021gdn, rasheed2026xaicps}. Across each detector, we compare \textbf{PI-CAX} against six XAI baselines: \textbf{Raw-MSE} \cite{fung2024ndss}, \textbf{Vanilla Gradient (VG)} \cite{oinonen2026epsr}, \textbf{Integrated Gradients (IG)} \cite{fung2024ndss, mathuros2024waxai}, \textbf{LIME} \cite{mathuros2024waxai, oinonen2026epsr}, \textbf{KernelSHAP} \cite{huong2022fedex, franco2026shats}, and \textbf{ShaTS} \cite{franco2026shats}.

\textbf{Key Empirical Findings from Tables~\ref{tab:xai_main}--\ref{tab:cross_detector}:}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Resolution of Mid-Attack Masking and Actuator Collapse (RQ1):} Observe in Table~\ref{tab:cross_detector} that \texttt{Raw-MSE} achieves $80.0\%$ Top-3 accuracy at the exact attack onset (\text{Onset@3} $= 0.8000$), when the initial step jump occurs, but collapses to $35.0\%\text{--}55.0\%$ during sustained mid-attack windows (\text{Mid@3}) as autoregressive self-lag weights mask the manipulated feature. By contrast, \textbf{PI-CAX} increases localization accuracy from $80.0\%$ at onset to \textbf{90.0\%--94.7\%} at mid-attack across all four detector families and achieves a \textbf{0.0000} normalized rank error on discrete actuator hijacks (vs. $0.474\text{--}0.630$ for LIME and KernelSHAP, and $0.084\text{--}0.200$ for ShaTS).
    \item \textbf{Superior Counterfactual Fidelity, Stability, and Adversarial Spoofing Resistance (RQ2):} Across all three datasets on \texttt{ST-GDN}, PI-CAX achieves the highest counterfactual comprehensiveness ($\text{Comp} = 0.7571$ vs. $0.3508$ for KernelSHAP and $0.6763$ for ShaTS), highest Hoyer sparsity ($0.8735$, preventing operator alert clutter), near-perfect noise stability ($0.9968$), and \textbf{84.47\%} Top-3 retention under $2.25\sigma$ adversarial attribution-spoofing decoys (whereas Raw-MSE drops to $6.93\%$ and IG/VG drop to $25.88\%\text{--}34.74\%$).
    \item \textbf{Sub-10 ms Edge Execution and Deadline Compliance (RQ3):} On the ARM Cortex-A72 edge profile, KernelSHAP ($105.35\text{ ms}$ on ST-GDN; $288.25\text{ ms}$ on GRU-Seq) and ShaTS ($82.05\text{ ms}$ on ST-GDN; $284.72\text{ ms}$ on GRU-Seq) completely miss the $10\text{ ms}$ and $50\text{ ms}$ deadlines ($\text{DCR}_{10} = 0.00$). PI-CAX executes in \textbf{1.06 ms} on desktop CPU and \textbf{6.78 ms} on the edge profile ($12.13\times$ faster than ShaTS and $15.54\times$ faster than KernelSHAP), achieving $\text{DCR}_{10} = 93.25\%$ and $\text{DCR}_{50} = 100.0\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations, Operational Deployment, and Reproducibility}

\textbf{Limitations:} (1) \emph{Stage Partition Knowledge:} Like ShaTS \cite{franco2026shats}, PI-CAX leverages plant topology mapping each tag (\texttt{FIT101}, \texttt{MV101}) to its physical process stage ($P_1\text{--}P_6$). In legacy brownfield plants without P\&ID documentation, stage clusters must first be inferred via spectral clustering of lag-1 transfer entropy. (2) \emph{Simultaneous Full-Stage Sensor Compromise:} If an omniscient adversary simultaneously compromises \emph{every} sensor and actuator within a stage $s^*$ to replay a prerecorded benign segment (Stuxnet-style full-stage replay), local cross-component residuals within $s^*$ will vanish until downstream stage $s^*+1$ diverges. (3) \emph{Non-Stationary Regime Drift:} Seasonal equipment wear requires periodic sliding-window updating of $\mathbf{A}_{\text{SCM}}$ and $\mathbf{B}_{\text{cross}}$ gated by ADWIN drift detectors \cite{skrodelis2026review}.

\textbf{Reproducibility Statement:} All benchmark generators, leakage-free chronological splits, detector implementations, baseline explainers, PI-CAX modules, statistical test scripts, and CSV outputs are packaged in our open-source repository (\texttt{xai\_cps\_repo/}) with deterministic seed control (\texttt{seed=42}) and automated unit tests (\texttt{tests/test\_framework.py}).
